What 'Standardised Extract' Means on an Herbal Label

Herbal supplement labels love the phrase "standardised extract" — as in "turmeric standardised to 95% curcuminoids" or "ashwagandha standardised to 5% withanolides". It sounds technical and reassuring. But what does standardisation actually guarantee, and what does it leave out?

A standardised extract is one where the manufacturer has adjusted the product so each batch contains a consistent percentage of a chosen marker compound. That is genuinely useful: plants vary by harvest, soil, and season, so without standardisation, one bottle could be far stronger or weaker than the next. Standardisation is how brands deliver a consistent dose.

The catch is what is chosen as the marker. The standardised compound is not always the compound responsible for the herb's effects — sometimes it is simply the easiest one to measure. A turmeric extract standardised to 95% curcuminoids sounds potent, but curcuminoids absorb poorly without piperine or fats, so the number alone does not tell you much about results.

Also watch the wording: "standardised to contain" is stronger than "standardised from" or "equivalent to". And standardisation says nothing about contaminants — heavy metals and pesticides need separate testing regardless of the percentage on the front.

Standardisation is a consistency tool, not a quality certificate. Read it alongside the rest of the label.
